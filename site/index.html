Built for iPhone

Your cameras, live, in one tap.

CamFetti is a free app for Thingino, ONVIF and RTSP cameras. You don't need an account or a subscription to watch cameras you already own, and the code is open source.

Get CamFetti Coming to the App Store. Free for every camera you have.

In your pocket. Every camera in one live grid. Swipe home and the one you're watching keeps playing in a corner.

On your wrist. Heard a knock? Raise your wrist to see the front door. Your iPhone fetches the picture, so camera passwords never leave it.

On your Mac. The same live grid in a window on your desk, and it finds cameras on your network the same way your iPhone does.

On your TV. Every camera on the big screen. Pick one with the remote and it fills the TV.

On Apple Vision Pro. Put the grid on your wall, and open any camera in a window of its own.

Finds your cameras

Thingino cameras show up by name as soon as you open the app, and most other brands turn up when it scans your Wi-Fi.

Fast to live

A Wyze on Thingino went live in under a quarter of a second in our tests. Reolink cameras took two to four, because they send a full picture less often.

Made for Thingino

Switch day and night mode, drag to pan a pan-and-tilt camera, and sign in with the password you already use for the camera's web page.

Works with most cameras

Reolink, Hikvision, Dahua, Amcrest and Tapo cameras work, along with anything else that speaks ONVIF or RTSP.

Picture in Picture

Swipe home and the nursery camera keeps playing in a corner while you answer a text.

Away from home

If you use Tailscale or another VPN, add the camera's away address once and it works anywhere. Video goes straight from the camera to your phone, never through our servers.

Free, for every camera

It's free on every Apple device it runs on, for as many cameras as you have, and there's a tip jar if you'd like to say thanks.

Collects nothing

CamFetti doesn't collect anything. Camera passwords stay in your device's Keychain, and the app only ever talks to your cameras.
